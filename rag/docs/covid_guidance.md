# COVID-19 (Coronavirus): Public Health Guidance

## Overview

COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 coronavirus. It primarily spreads through airborne respiratory droplets and smaller aerosols when an infected individual breathes, speaks, coughs, or sneezes. The disease ranges from mild cold-like symptoms to severe viral pneumonia and acute respiratory distress.

## COVID-19 Vaccine Names and Types

Multiple vaccines have been approved by global health authorities (including the WHO, FDA, and EMA) to prevent severe COVID-19:

1. **Pfizer-BioNTech (Comirnaty)**: An mRNA vaccine encoding the viral spike protein, approved for primary series and updated seasonal boosters.
2. **Moderna (Spikevax)**: An mRNA vaccine using lipid nanoparticle technology, widely authorized for children and adults.
3. **Novavax (Nuvaxovid / Covovax)**: A protein subunit vaccine with a saponin-based adjuvant (Matrix-M), providing an alternative to mRNA technology.
4. **Oxford-AstraZeneca (Vaxzevria / Covishield)**: A recombinant adenovirus vector vaccine.
5. **Johnson & Johnson (Janssen)**: A single-dose adenovirus vector vaccine (Ad26.COV2.S).
6. **Bharat Biotech (Covaxin)**: An inactivated whole-virion coronavirus vaccine.
7. **Sinovac (CoronaVac)** and **Sinopharm (BBIBP-CorV)**: Inactivated virus vaccines widely used globally.

## Vaccination and Boosters

Vaccination is the most effective intervention to prevent severe illness, hospitalization, and death from COVID-19.
- **Immunity Timeline**: Substantial antibody protection develops approximately 10 to 14 days following vaccination.
- **Boosters**: Because the virus evolves and antibodies decline over time, updated seasonal booster shots tailored to recent circulating variants (such as Omicron sublineages) are recommended, particularly for older adults, healthcare personnel, and individuals with underlying health conditions.

## Symptoms

Common COVID-19 symptoms include fever or chills, dry cough, shortness of breath, sudden loss of taste or smell (anosmia), extreme fatigue, body aches, headache, sore throat, and nasal congestion. Symptoms typically emerge 2 to 14 days after exposure.

## When to Seek Emergency Medical Attention

Seek immediate emergency medical care if you observe red-flag symptoms: trouble breathing, persistent pain or pressure in the chest, new confusion, inability to wake or stay awake, or pale, gray, or blue-colored skin, lips, or nail beds.

## Everyday Prevention Measures

In addition to vaccination, transmission risk is significantly reduced by improving indoor ventilation, wearing high-filtration masks (such as N95 or KN95 respirators) in crowded spaces, practicing frequent hand hygiene with soap or alcohol-based sanitizer, and isolating when symptomatic.
