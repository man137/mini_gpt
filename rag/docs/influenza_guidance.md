# Seasonal Influenza: Public Health Guidance

## Overview

Seasonal influenza (the flu) is an acute respiratory infection caused by
influenza viruses. It spreads mainly through respiratory droplets released
when an infected person coughs, sneezes or talks, and less often through
contaminated surfaces. Most people recover within one to two weeks without
medical treatment, but certain groups are at higher risk of severe illness.

## Symptoms

Typical symptoms appear suddenly and include fever, chills, muscle and joint
pain, headache, sore throat, dry cough and severe tiredness. Fever usually
lasts three to five days. Children may also experience gastrointestinal
symptoms such as nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea, which are uncommon in adults.

## Contagious Period

An adult with influenza is contagious from about one day before symptoms
begin until five to seven days after onset. Children and people with weakened
immune systems can shed the virus for longer, sometimes more than ten days.
The highest viral shedding usually coincides with the fever period, which is
why staying home until 24 hours after the fever resolves without
fever-reducing medication is recommended.

## Vaccination

Annual vaccination is the most effective way to prevent influenza. The
vaccine is reformulated each year to match circulating strains. When the
match is good, vaccination reduces the risk of flu illness by approximately
40 to 60 percent in the general population. Immunity takes about two weeks
to develop after vaccination. Vaccination is especially important for
pregnant women, adults over 65, young children, and people with chronic
conditions such as asthma, diabetes or heart disease.

## When to Seek Medical Care

Most cases can be managed at home with rest and fluids. Seek medical care
promptly if there is difficulty breathing, chest pain, persistent high fever
beyond three days, confusion, signs of dehydration, or if symptoms improve
and then return with a worse cough and fever, which can signal a secondary
bacterial infection such as pneumonia.

## Hygiene Measures

Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or elbow, wash hands with soap and
water for at least 20 seconds, use alcohol-based hand sanitiser with at
least 60 percent alcohol when soap is unavailable, and ventilate indoor
spaces regularly during flu season.
